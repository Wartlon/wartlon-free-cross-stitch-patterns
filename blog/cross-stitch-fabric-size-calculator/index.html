How to calculate cross stitch fabric size

The basic calculation is simple: divide the number of stitches by the number of stitches that fit into one inch of fabric.

Design size in inches = number of stitches ÷ effective fabric count

For example, a pattern that is 140 stitches wide on 14-count Aida will be 10 inches wide. The same 140-stitch design on 18-count Aida will be about 7.8 inches wide.

Remember: the finished design size and the fabric cut size are not the same thing. You need extra fabric around the stitched area for a hoop, framing, finishing and handling.

What does Aida count mean?

Aida count tells you how many cross-stitch squares fit into one inch of fabric. On 14-count Aida, there are 14 squares per inch. On 18-count Aida, there are 18. A higher count produces smaller stitches and a smaller finished design.

What about evenweave and linen?

Evenweave and linen are often stitched over two fabric threads rather than over one. If you stitch over two on 28-count fabric, the effective cross-stitch count is 14 stitches per inch. That means a design will come out approximately the same size as it would on 14-count Aida.

In the calculator, choose “2 fabric threads” if you are stitching over two.

How much extra fabric should I add?

There is no single margin that suits every project. A common choice is around 2–3 inches (5–7.5 cm) on each side. Three inches per side gives you six extra inches total in both width and height.

You may want more margin if the piece will be professionally framed, if you prefer plenty of fabric in a hoop or Q-snap, or if you have not decided how you will finish the project yet. Very small ornaments and pin keeps may need less.

Example: 80 × 120 stitches on 14-count Aida

An 80-stitch width on 14-count Aida is about 5.7 inches. A 120-stitch height is about 8.6 inches. With a 3-inch margin on every side, you would want a fabric cut of roughly 11.7 × 14.6 inches before rounding up.

The calculator rounds the recommended cut upward so you do not accidentally end up short.

Does changing fabric count change the pattern?

No. The number and placement of stitches stay the same. Changing the fabric count changes the physical size of each stitch, so the finished embroidery becomes larger or smaller.

This is useful when you want to make a mini pattern slightly larger, fit a design into a particular frame, or compare how the same chart will look on 14-, 16- and 18-count Aida.

Before you cut your fabric

  • Check that your pattern dimensions are listed in stitches, not centimeters or inches.
  • Confirm the fabric count printed on the fabric packaging.
  • If using linen or evenweave, decide whether you will stitch over one or over two threads.
  • Add enough margin for your preferred finishing method.
  • When in doubt, cutting a little more fabric is safer than cutting too close to the design.

Quick FAQ

How big is 100 stitches on 14-count Aida?

100 stitches divided by 14 is about 7.1 inches, or approximately 18.1 cm.

How big is 100 stitches on 18-count Aida?

100 stitches divided by 18 is about 5.6 inches, or approximately 14.1 cm.

Is 28-count evenweave the same size as 14-count Aida?

If you stitch over two threads on 28-count evenweave, the effective count is 14 stitches per inch, so the finished design size is approximately the same as on 14-count Aida.

Should I include the margin when measuring the finished design?

No. “Finished design size” refers only to the stitched area. The fabric cut should be larger because it includes extra unstitched fabric around the design.
