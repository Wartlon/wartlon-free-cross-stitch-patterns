How to Start Cross Stitching for Beginners

Cross stitch is one of the easiest forms of counted embroidery to begin: follow a grid, make X-shaped stitches and build the picture one square at a time.

Published August 31, 2026

What do you need?

  • Cross stitch pattern
  • Aida or another embroidery fabric
  • Embroidery floss
  • Tapestry needle
  • Embroidery scissors
  • Hoop or other holder — optional

1. Choose a small first pattern

A small design with a limited palette lets you practice counting without committing to a huge project. Check stitch count, color key and instructions first.

Learn how to read a cross stitch pattern →

2. Choose your fabric

Aida has visible squares. Its count tells you how many squares fit into an inch; 14-count Aida has 14. Fabric count changes the physical size of the finished embroidery.

Calculate the fabric size → · What does 14-count Aida mean? →

3. Prepare the fabric

Leave blank fabric around the whole design for handling and finishing. If starting from the center, locate the midpoint by gently folding or measuring horizontally and vertically.

4. Prepare the floss

Embroidery floss is made of separable strands. Follow the pattern recommendation for how many strands to use. Cut a manageable length, separate the strands and thread the tapestry needle. Very long floss tangles and wears more easily.

5. Make your first cross stitch

Bring the needle from back to front through one corner of an Aida square and take it down through the diagonally opposite corner. Make the second diagonal across it to complete the X.

Neatness tip: keep the top diagonal of your crosses facing the same direction and avoid pulling so tightly that the fabric distorts.

6. Stitch rows consistently

For adjacent stitches of one color, you can make a row of first diagonals and work back to complete the crosses, or complete each X individually. Consistency matters more than speed.

7. Secure the thread

A common finishing method is to run the needle under several existing stitches on the back and trim the excess. There are several valid starting methods too, including securing a short tail or using a loop start when your strand setup allows it.

8. Check your counting

Many beginner errors are simply correct stitches placed in the wrong square. Count from stitches you trust and double-check before jumping across a large blank area.

9. Finish your first project

After stitching, check for loose threads. Your piece can then be prepared for a frame, ornament, pin keep, card or another small finish appropriate to the materials.

A simple first-project plan

  1. Pick a small full-cross pattern.
  2. Choose an easy-to-see fabric such as 14-count Aida.
  3. Calculate the fabric cut and margin.
  4. Sort floss by DMC number.
  5. Read the key before stitching.
  6. Work in a small area first.
  7. Check your count regularly.

Beginner FAQ

Is cross stitch difficult?

The basic full cross is simple. The main skills are reading the chart, counting accurately and keeping stitches consistent.

What fabric should a beginner use?

Aida is common because its square structure makes placement easy to see. 14 count is a popular all-purpose option.

Do I need a hoop?

No. It is a preference. Some stitchers like the tension; others stitch in hand or use another frame.

Where should I start?

The center is common, but not mandatory. Use a method that lets you place the first stitch accurately.
